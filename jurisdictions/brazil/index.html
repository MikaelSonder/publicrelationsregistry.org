Public Relations & Communications in Brazil is the commercial service-line concerned with corporate reputation, media relations, stakeholder engagement, public affairs, executive positioning, crisis and issues management, investor communication, internal communication and digital content. It is delivered by specialist PR consultancies, corporate affairs and public affairs advisers, integrated agencies, in-house communications departments, influencer agencies and Brazilian offices or affiliates of international communications networks.

Brazil is a large, federal, regional and digitally intensive communications market. São Paulo is the principal centre for corporate headquarters, financial markets, consumer brands, media, advertising and agency activity; Brasília is central to the federal government, Congress, regulators and public affairs; Rio de Janeiro is material to energy, media, cultural and corporate communication; and Belo Horizonte, Porto Alegre, Curitiba, Recife, Salvador, Manaus and other cities can be important for regional business, industry, agriculture, infrastructure, consumer and community stakeholder work. Portuguese is indispensable to domestic credibility and content governance.

Brazil has an unusual professional dimension: public relations is a regulated profession under Law No. 5,377/1967 and Federal Decree No. 63,283/1968. The Federal Council of Public Relations Professionals (CONFERP) and regional councils govern the exercise of the profession by legally accredited individuals and entities. Professional ethics are set through the Code of Ethics for Public Relations Professionals. Commercial advertising is governed by the Consumer Defense Code and self-regulated through CONAR. CONAR’s 2026 Guide on Marketing and Advertising by Digital Influencers requires clear, immediate and prominent identification of advertising content, with appropriate platform tools or labels such as “#publicidade” and “#publi.”

For international businesses, Brazilian PR and communications work should be planned around São Paulo corporate and financial communications, Brasília public affairs, Portuguese-language delivery, CONFERP professional registration relevance, CONAR influencer advertising controls, LGPD data governance, sector-specific consumer claims and rapid crisis management. Agency appointment is commercial, but in Brazil the professional activity and its digital advertising formats can each have distinct regulatory implications that should be assessed before operation.

Process Flow and Decision Tree

No single statutory workflow governs every Brazilian communications engagement. A well-run mandate begins with business, professional registration, federal-state, stakeholder, language and regulatory analysis, then proceeds to strategy, Portuguese-language material development, approval, execution, monitoring and reporting. Consumer, influencer, data-driven, financial and regulated PR activity requires early classification. The provider should identify whether CONFERP registration is relevant and whether advertising content meets CONAR’s definition of influencer advertising based on promotion, reciprocal commitments and advertiser editorial control.

2. Map Professional, Federal and Market StakeholdersIdentify relevant CONFERP or regional council registration questions, Brasília and state institutions, São Paulo and regional media, regulators, investors, associations, consumers, creators, employees and communities.

5. 6. Produce MaterialsDevelop Portuguese media materials, executive briefings, public affairs documents, investor messages, campaign content, creator agreements, CONAR disclosures, claims files and LGPD records.

Decision logic: Start with the business objective, Brazilian target audience, state and city exposure, sector and channel. Then determine whether legally regulated public relations activity is involved and whether the provider has appropriate professional registration. For creator content, test for promotion, reciprocal commitment and advertiser editorial control. If the activity is influencer advertising, identify it clearly and immediately using platform tools and a suitable Portuguese disclosure such as “#publicidade” or “#publi,” integrated into the content and visible without clicking “mais conteúdo.”

Operating Constraints, Risks and Costs

The central practical risk is treating Brazil as only a Portuguese translation market or a standard Latin American extension. Credible delivery requires professional registration analysis where activities fall within regulated PR scope, Portuguese-language quality, São Paulo and Brasília planning, regional stakeholder engagement, transparent creator relationships, substantiated consumer claims and LGPD data governance. CONAR’s current guidance expects advertising disclosure to be clear, immediate, prominent and visible without additional interaction; vague tags such as “#partner” or “#collab” may not clearly convey the commercial relationship.

Professional Registration RiskPublic relations is a regulated profession in Brazil. Providers, clients and foreign agencies should assess whether the engagement includes reserved professional activities and whether individuals or legal entities require CONFERP or regional council registration.
Influencer Advertising RiskWhere a creator promotes a product, service, cause or brand under reciprocal commitments with the advertiser or agency and the advertiser has editorial control, the content is influencer advertising and must be clearly identified.
Disclosure Placement RiskAdvertising identification should be clear, immediate, prominent and integrated into content, visible without clicking “mais conteúdo.” Appropriate labels include “#publicidade” or “#publi” and relevant platform paid partnership functionality. Vague labels such as “#ambassador,” “#partner,” “#collaboration” or “#colab” may be insufficient.
Children and Teenagers RiskCommercial content that can be recognised by children or teenagers requires heightened care. Written disclosure, appropriate visual and verbal signals for audio or video, and careful assessment of product category and persuasive techniques are required.
Federal and Regional Stakeholder RiskA São Paulo or Brasília-only communications plan can overlook state governments, municipal authorities, regional media, local communities, workforce, environmental, agribusiness, industrial or tourism interests material to the actual investment or issue.

FAQ

Is public relations a regulated profession in Brazil?Yes. Brazil regulates the public relations profession under Law No. 5,377/1967 and Federal Decree No. 63,283/1968. The Federal Council of Public Relations Professionals (CONFERP) and regional councils oversee the professional framework. The specific activity, practitioner or entity should be assessed for registration requirements.
What is CONAR?CONAR is the Conselho Nacional de Autorregulamentação Publicitária, Brazil's advertising self-regulatory body. It administers the Brazilian Advertising Self-Regulation Code and publishes guidance on advertising and marketing by digital influencers.
When is creator content treated as influencer advertising?CONAR identifies three cumulative features: the content promotes a product, service, cause, brand or associated sign; it arises from reciprocal commitments, including payment, commission, benefits or another material connection; and the advertiser or agency has editorial control over the content.
How should influencer advertising be labelled in Brazil?Use clear, immediate and prominent identification, visible without clicking “mais conteúdo,” through platform paid partnership functionality and/or labels such as “#publicidade” or “#publi.” The disclosure should be integrated into the content and fit the format; video, live and audio communications can require visible and verbal identification. Vague terms such as “#partner” or “#collab” should not be relied on alone.
Does LGPD apply to direct marketing?Yes. LGPD governs personal data used in CRM, direct marketing, audience segmentation, targeting, analytics and data sharing. While it does not create a standalone direct-marketing regime, data subjects have privacy rights and organisations should document an appropriate legal basis, transparency, opt-out or objection process, security and vendor controls before marketing use.
