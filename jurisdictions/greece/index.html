Public Relations & Communications in Greece is the commercial service-line concerned with corporate reputation, media relations, public affairs, stakeholder engagement, executive positioning, crisis and issues management, investor communications, internal communication and digital content. It is delivered through specialist communications consultancies, public affairs advisers, integrated agencies, in-house corporate communications teams and Greek offices or partners of international agency networks.

The Greek market is centred strongly on Athens for national government, Parliament, national media, corporate headquarters, finance, public affairs and agency activity. Thessaloniki is the major northern commercial, university and regional centre, while tourism, shipping, energy, infrastructure, real estate and island or local-community contexts can materially change the stakeholder environment. A credible Greek mandate requires Greek-language delivery, realistic Athens policy and media analysis and a tailored approach to regional, sectoral and community communications where operations or public issues extend beyond the capital.

Public relations is not a licensed profession in Greece. The commercial and ethical environment is shaped by the Greek Advertising and Communication Code administered through the Greek Advertising Self-Regulation Council (SEE). Its Influencer Marketing Annex sets specific ethical principles on compensation, content control, recognisability, disclosure and the use of filters or edited images. Sponsored influencer content must be clearly identifiable as advertising; the disclosure should be explicit, promptly visible, clear, prominent and placed at the beginning of relevant posts or videos, using Greek and/or English wording appropriate to the audience.

For international businesses, Greek PR and communications work should be planned around Athens public affairs, Greek-language content, regional and sector stakeholder relevance, advertising and influencer transparency, GDPR and Hellenic Data Protection Authority requirements, direct-marketing consent and crisis governance. Agency appointment is a commercial engagement rather than a licensing process, but local language, transparent commercial communication, public-policy awareness and disciplined approval processes directly shape delivery and reputational risk.

Process Flow and Decision Tree

No uniform statutory workflow governs Greek PR or communications engagements. A typical mandate moves from client, stakeholder and market analysis to strategy, Greek-language content development, execution, commercial and privacy controls, monitoring and reporting. Consumer, influencer and direct electronic marketing activity requires a separate early compliance review covering commercial intent, consideration, content control, clear disclosure, consent, sender identity, opt-out and recordkeeping requirements.

1. Define the ObjectiveIdentify the corporate, commercial, investor, public-policy, market-entry or reputational objective, target audiences, national or regional scope, channels, timeline and required service category.
2. Map Greek StakeholdersIdentify relevant Athens and regional media, government and local institutions, regulators, associations, investors, consumer audiences, creators, employees and communities.
3. Select Provider and Engagement ModelDetermine whether the mandate requires corporate communications, Athens public affairs, media relations, tourism or shipping support, regional stakeholder work, consumer or influencer compliance, direct-marketing controls, crisis or integrated international capability.
4. Develop Strategy and MessagingPrepare Greek-market positioning, narrative, factual support, key messages, stakeholder plan, language approach, approval paths, disclosure approach and reputational risk scenarios.
5. Classify Commercial Content and Direct MarketingDetermine whether the material is advertising, sponsored or influenced content, self-promotion or direct electronic marketing; identify compensation, content control, labels, platform tools, consent, sender identity, opt-out and documentation requirements.
6. Produce MaterialsDevelop media materials, executive briefings, public affairs documents, Greek content, creator instructions, disclosure wording, contracts, direct-marketing copy and privacy records where relevant.
7. Execute and EngageConduct media relations, public affairs, corporate and investor announcements, consumer campaigns, influencer activity, compliant electronic outreach, events, stakeholder dialogue or crisis response.
8. Monitor and ReportMonitor media, policy, stakeholder, consumer and social response, including commercial transparency, claims, consent, opt-outs, privacy and campaign performance indicators.
9. Review Risk and ChangeUpdate strategy, messages, labels, approvals, data controls and crisis planning as corporate events, regulation, regional conditions, public debate or campaign performance evolves.
Decision logic: Start with the objective, audience, national or regional scope and communications channel. Then select the suitable Greek service-line. If commercial content is involved, use an explicit, clear and prominent disclosure at the beginning of the post or video, supported by relevant platform tools. If electronic direct marketing is planned, establish prior explicit consent or a valid existing-customer exception, identify the sender and provide a free, effective opt-out route before outreach.

Operating Constraints, Risks and Costs

The central practical risk is treating Greece as a simple translated extension of a wider Mediterranean or European campaign. Credible delivery requires Greek-language quality, Athens and regional stakeholder mapping, visible commercial disclosure, documented creator relationships and disciplined data governance. SEE guidance expects commercial intent to be disclosed promptly and distinctly at the beginning of a post or video, using clear wording and relevant platform tools. Direct electronic marketing requires prior express consent except where a limited existing-customer exception applies.

Commercial Disclosure RiskInfluencer or sponsored content that does not state its commercial nature clearly, promptly and prominently at the beginning of a post, video or accompanying text can create self-regulatory, consumer and reputational exposure.
Compensation and Content Control RiskPayment, free goods, discounts, travel, invitations, affiliate arrangements or advertiser content control should be documented and reflected in disclosure, approval and responsibility arrangements.
Language and Localisation RiskEnglish-only or generic regional content may fail to engage Greek domestic audiences, media, policymakers, local communities or consumers and can make advertising disclosures less effective.
Direct Marketing Consent RiskUnsolicited marketing by electronic communication generally requires prior express consent. Messages must identify the sender or advertiser and allow recipients to opt out from future communications.
Regional Stakeholder RiskAn Athens-only communications plan can miss regional, island, port, municipality, tourism, shipping, infrastructure or community stakeholders relevant to an investment, project or corporate issue.
Claims and Data Protection RiskEnvironmental, sustainability, product, health, financial, price and performance claims need substantiation where relevant; media, stakeholder, customer and creator data require GDPR-compliant governance.

FAQ

Is public relations a regulated profession in Greece?No. PR practitioners do not require a dedicated statutory licence. The field is subject to applicable advertising self-regulation, consumer, data protection, electronic communications, audiovisual and sector-specific rules.
What is the SEE Influencer Marketing Annex?It is an annex to the Greek Advertising and Communication Code adopted by the Greek Advertising Self-Regulation Council. It sets ethical principles for influencer marketing, including compensation, content control, recognisability, disclosure, responsibility and use of filters or edited images.
How should influencer advertising be disclosed in Greece?The commercial nature should be stated explicitly, clearly and prominently at the beginning of a post or video and in accompanying content where relevant. The disclosure may use clear Greek and/or English wording such as “#advertisement” or “#ad” and can be supported by platform “paid partnership” tools.
Can a company send marketing emails without consent in Greece?For unsolicited direct marketing by email, SMS, automated calls and other electronic communications, prior explicit consent is generally required under Law 3471/2006, subject to a limited existing-customer exception. The sender must be identifiable and recipients must be able to opt out.
Can a foreign company appoint a Greek PR agency directly?Yes. Agency appointment is normally a commercial contract matter. The mandate should define Greek-language authority, Athens and regional coverage, commercial-content controls, direct-marketing and privacy roles, sector expertise and coordination with international teams.
