Public Relations & Communications in Texas is the commercial service-line concerned with corporate reputation, media relations, public affairs, stakeholder engagement, executive positioning, crisis and issues management, investor communications, internal communication and digital content. It is delivered by specialist PR consultancies, corporate affairs firms, public affairs advisers, integrated agencies, in-house communications teams, investor relations advisers, digital and influencer agencies and Texas offices of national and global communications networks.

Texas is a large, economically diverse and regionally differentiated state communications market. Austin is the centre for Texas state government, legislative affairs, regulatory engagement, technology and public policy; Dallas-Fort Worth is a major corporate headquarters, financial, professional services, media and consumer market; Houston is central to energy, industrial, healthcare, port, trade and global corporate communication; San Antonio has major public sector, defence, healthcare, tourism and regional business relevance; and other regions can be material for manufacturing, agriculture, logistics, infrastructure, energy and community stakeholder engagement. A statewide mandate should distinguish these markets rather than assuming a single Texas audience.

Public relations is not a Texas-licensed profession. Professional ethics are informed by the national PRSA Code of Ethics and Texas PRSA chapter practice. Commercial communications are governed by federal FTC endorsement principles and Texas consumer protection law, principally the Texas Deceptive Trade Practices-Consumer Protection Act (DTPA). Advertisers and endorsers must ensure that endorsements reflect genuine opinions, findings, beliefs and experiences; paid or benefit-based connections that consumers would not expect must be disclosed clearly and conspicuously. Texas has also enacted the Texas Data Privacy and Security Act (TDPSA), effective 1 July 2024, creating consumer rights concerning personal data, including opt-outs from targeted advertising, sales of personal data and certain profiling.

For international businesses, Texas communications work should be planned around Austin public affairs, Dallas corporate communication, Houston energy and industrial stakeholders, Texas consumer protection, FTC endorsement disclosure, TDPSA privacy, CAN-SPAM, TCPA and Do Not Call rules, sector-specific claims and crisis governance. Agency appointment is commercial rather than licensed, but Texas state law, local market differences, data rights, sector regulation and community context require a detailed local operating model.

Operating Constraints, Risks and Costs

The central practical risk is treating Texas as either a single regional US market or an extension of another state. Texas combines major corporate, energy, technology, financial, public affairs and consumer ecosystems with distinct state privacy and consumer enforcement. A credible mandate requires Austin, Dallas, Houston and regional stakeholder analysis; clear material connection disclosure; robust claims substantiation; documented TDPSA notice and opt-out controls; and a sector-aware response model for highly visible commercial, regulatory or operational issues.

Material Connection RiskPayment, free or discounted products or services, affiliate commissions, employment, family and personal relationships can be material connections. When consumers would not expect the connection, it must be clearly and conspicuously disclosed with the endorsement.
Endorsement Truthfulness RiskEven where sponsorship is disclosed, an endorsement must reflect the endorser's honest opinions, findings, beliefs or experience. Brands and agencies should verify claims, actual use and testimonial wording before distribution.
Texas Privacy RiskQualifying businesses processing Texas consumer data must assess TDPSA rights, privacy notice, collection limitation, targeted advertising, sale, profiling, opt-outs, appeal process, contracts, data security and Attorney General enforcement risk.
Consumer Claims and Litigation RiskFalse, misleading or deceptive product, environmental, sustainability, health, safety, financial, price, performance, comparative and testimonial claims can create DTPA enforcement and private litigation exposure. Claims require documentary substantiation before publication.
Austin, Regional and Community RiskAn Austin, Dallas or Houston-only plan can overlook state and local government, regional regulators, workforce, communities, environmental interests and local media that are material to energy, infrastructure, technology, healthcare, property and operational projects.
Email, Text and Calling RiskCommercial email, SMS and telemarketing involve distinct federal and state frameworks. CAN-SPAM, TCPA, Do Not Call, TDPSA and Texas consumer law require channel-specific analysis rather than a generic marketing consent model.
Energy, Technology and Financial Sector RiskEnergy, industrial, environmental, technology, AI, financial, consumer financial, health and investor communications can each have specific state or federal claims, disclosure, data, licensing or regulator engagement requirements.

FAQ

Is public relations a regulated profession in Texas?No. Texas does not require a separate PR professional licence. Professional ethics are voluntary and informed by PRSA. Particular communications activities can be governed by Texas and federal consumer protection, advertising, privacy, direct marketing, lobbying, financial and sector-specific rules.
Why do Austin, Dallas and Houston require different communications approaches?Austin is the Texas state government and public affairs centre; Dallas-Fort Worth is a major corporate, financial, professional services and media market; Houston is central to energy, industrial, port and global corporate communications. Each has different stakeholders, media, investors, regulators and reputational conditions, so statewide work may need dedicated workstreams.
Do Texas influencers need to disclose gifts and affiliate links?Yes where there is a material connection that consumers would not expect. Under FTC Endorsement Guides, this can include payment, free products, discounts, services, travel, affiliate commissions, employment, family or personal relationships. The disclosure should be clear, conspicuous and placed with the endorsement.
Does TDPSA affect communications and marketing?Yes, for qualifying organisations. TDPSA gives Texas consumers rights regarding personal data and includes opt-out rights for targeted advertising, sale of personal data and certain profiling. Organisations should assess whether they are covered and implement the required privacy notice, rights and security processes before using Texas consumer data in campaigns.
Can a company send commercial email, text or telemarketing to Texans without consent?The answer depends on the channel. Commercial email is governed by CAN-SPAM, which generally requires accurate sender information, a postal address and a functional opt-out rather than prior consent. Text messages and telemarketing can trigger TCPA, Do Not Call and state-law requirements, where consent may be needed. A channel-specific legal review is required.
