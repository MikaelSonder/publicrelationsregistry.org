Public Relations & Communications in Florida is the commercial service-line concerned with corporate reputation, media relations, public affairs, stakeholder engagement, executive positioning, crisis and issues management, investor communications, internal communication and digital content. It is delivered by specialist PR consultancies, corporate affairs firms, public affairs advisers, integrated agencies, in-house communications teams, investor relations advisers, digital and influencer agencies and Florida offices of national and global communications networks.

Florida is a large, internationally connected and regionally diverse state communications market. Miami and South Florida are major centres for international business, Latin American and Caribbean connections, finance, real estate, hospitality, aviation, consumer brands, media, entertainment and creator activity. Tallahassee is the state government, legislature, regulatory and public affairs centre. Orlando is material for tourism, entertainment, conventions and technology; Tampa Bay for corporate, healthcare, financial, technology and media communications; Jacksonville for logistics, financial services, healthcare and regional business; and coastal, agricultural, energy and climate-exposed regions can require dedicated community and stakeholder engagement.

Public relations is not a Florida-licensed profession. The Florida Public Relations Association (FPRA) provides a state professional ethics framework requiring public interest, honesty, integrity, accuracy, disclosure of represented clients and interests, avoidance of deceptive practices, confidentiality and respect for fair competition. Commercial communications are governed by federal FTC endorsement principles and Florida’s Deceptive and Unfair Trade Practices Act (FDUTPA). Florida has also enacted the Digital Bill of Rights, which establishes privacy rights and obligations for qualifying businesses, including consumer opt-outs from the sale of personal data, targeted advertising and certain profiling.

For international businesses, Florida communications work should be planned around Miami international and bilingual markets, Tallahassee public affairs, regional and community stakeholder context, FTC endorsement disclosure, FDUTPA consumer claims, Florida Digital Bill of Rights privacy, CAN-SPAM, TCPA and Do Not Call obligations, sector-specific review and crisis governance. Agency appointment is commercial rather than licensed, but Florida’s tourism, real estate, consumer, technology, climate, international trade and privacy environments require a locally designed operating model.

Operating Constraints, Risks and Costs

The central practical risk is treating Florida as a single coastal consumer market or as an undifferentiated extension of a US campaign. Florida combines international Miami markets, Tallahassee state policy, substantial tourism and real estate activity, climate and coastal exposure, diverse language audiences, strong consumer protection and qualifying state privacy obligations. A credible mandate requires local stakeholder analysis, clear material connection disclosure, robust claims support, documented privacy and direct-marketing controls and a responsive model for high-visibility consumer, environmental, project or crisis issues.

Material Connection RiskPayment, free or discounted products and services, affiliate commissions, employment, family and personal relationships can be material connections. When consumers would not expect the connection, it must be clearly and conspicuously disclosed with the endorsement.
Creator Monitoring RiskBrands and agencies should train, contractually require, monitor and correct creator disclosures. Brand tags, vague relationship language, buried hashtags or a statement in an agreement are not sufficient operating controls without live-content review.
Florida Privacy RiskQualifying businesses processing Florida consumer data should assess Florida Digital Bill of Rights scope, notice, rights, targeted advertising, sale, profiling, opt-out mechanisms, processors, retention, security and Attorney General enforcement risk.

Claims require documentary substantiation before publication.

FAQ

Is public relations a regulated profession in Florida?No. Florida does not require a separate PR professional licence. Professional ethics are supported by FPRA, while communications activity is subject to applicable federal and state consumer protection, advertising, privacy, direct-marketing, lobbying, financial and sector-specific rules.
Why do Miami and Tallahassee require different communications approaches?Miami is a major international, Latin American, Caribbean, finance, tourism, real estate, consumer and media centre. Tallahassee is the Florida state government, legislative, regulatory and lobbying hub. A statewide mandate often requires separate media, stakeholder, language, public affairs and approval workstreams.
Do Florida influencers need to disclose gifts and affiliate links?Yes where there is a material connection that consumers would not expect. Under FTC Endorsement Guides, this can include payment, free products, discounts, services, travel, affiliate commissions, employment, family or personal relationships. The disclosure should be clear, conspicuous and placed with the endorsement.
Does the Florida Digital Bill of Rights affect communications and marketing?It can, for qualifying controllers. The Act creates consumer rights and obligations for certain Florida consumer data processing, including rights to opt out of targeted advertising, sale of personal data and certain profiling. Organisations should assess scope, privacy notices, consumer rights, processors and opt-out mechanisms before using Florida consumer data in a campaign.
Can a company send commercial email, text or telemarketing to Floridians without consent?The answer depends on the channel. Commercial email is governed by CAN-SPAM, which generally requires accurate sender information, a postal address and a functional opt-out. Text messages and telemarketing can trigger TCPA, Do Not Call and state-law requirements, where consent may be required. Channel-specific analysis is necessary.
