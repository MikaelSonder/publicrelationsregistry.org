Public Relations & Communications in China is the commercial service-line concerned with corporate reputation, media and platform relations, stakeholder engagement, government and public affairs, executive positioning, crisis and issues management, investor communication, internal communication and digital content. It is delivered by specialist PR consultancies, corporate affairs advisers, public affairs firms, digital and KOL agencies, in-house communications teams and China offices of international agency networks.

China is a large, regionalised and platform-driven communications market. Beijing is central to national government, policy, regulators, state and national media, public affairs and corporate headquarters; Shanghai is a major commercial, financial, consumer, luxury and international business hub; Shenzhen and Guangzhou are key technology, consumer, manufacturing and Greater Bay Area centres; and regional cities can be central to industrial, local government, commercial and community stakeholder work. Mandarin Chinese content, local platform operation and direct management of government, media and digital stakeholder relationships are fundamental to credible execution.

Public relations is not a licensed profession as a separate regulated service-line. Its practical environment is nevertheless tightly shaped by advertising, consumer, internet, data, media and sectoral regulation. The State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) administers the Advertising Law and the Measures for the Administration of Internet Advertising, effective from 1 May 2023. Internet advertising must be recognisable and prominently marked “广告” (advertisement). Key Opinion Leader (KOL), livestream and creator content that directly or indirectly promotes goods or services can be treated as internet advertising when a commercial relationship exists. Advertisers, agencies, endorsers and platforms can each have material compliance responsibilities.

For international businesses, China communications work should be planned around Chinese-language narrative, local platforms, Beijing policy relationships, city and regional stakeholder structures, SAMR advertising controls, Personal Information Protection Law (PIPL) requirements, cross-border data issues, sectoral claims and crisis governance. Agency appointment is commercial rather than licensed, but foreign content, platforms, approval processes and campaign governance cannot simply be transferred into China without local operational and legal adaptation.

Country Characteristics

China's communications environment is characterised by its scale, regional diversity, platform-specific digital ecosystem, close interaction between commercial activity and government or regulatory context, and high importance of Chinese-language content. Beijing, Shanghai, Shenzhen and Guangzhou have distinct corporate, media, policy, technology and consumer roles. Effective public relations requires local fact control, stakeholder sequencing, platform adaptation and clear separation of corporate communications, editorial-style content and commercial advertising. A communications plan must also account for the responsibility of advertisers, agencies, endorsers, KOLs and platforms under China’s advertising and internet governance rules.

Applicable Legislation

China does not impose a separate PR licence. The applicable framework is distributed across the Advertising Law, internet advertising rules, consumer and anti-unfair competition law, personal information and cybersecurity regulation, content and platform rules, financial regulation and sector-specific requirements. The early practical question is whether content directly or indirectly markets a product or service through an internet medium. If it does, it may be internet advertising and must be recognisable, prominently labelled and supported by appropriate advertiser, agency, KOL and platform controls.

Process Flow and Decision Tree

No uniform statutory workflow governs China PR or communications engagements. A well-run mandate begins with business, regulatory, city and stakeholder analysis, then moves to strategy, Chinese-language content development, internal and local approvals, execution, platform monitoring and reporting. Consumer, KOL and digital campaigns require an additional early advertising and data classification: the advertiser should determine whether content is internet advertising, whether it must carry “广告”, what claims may be made, who owns approval and how data and platform functions will be controlled.

1. Define the ObjectiveIdentify the corporate, consumer, investor, public-policy, market-entry or reputational objective, target audiences, cities, platforms, sector, timeline, internal owner and required service category.
2. Map China Stakeholders and Market CentresIdentify relevant Beijing, Shanghai, Greater Bay Area and regional media, government and local authorities, regulators, platforms, investors, customers, KOLs, employees, associations and communities.
3. Select Provider and Engagement ModelDetermine whether the mandate requires corporate communications, public affairs, media and platform relations, financial communications, KOL governance, regional stakeholder work, crisis support or integrated China and Asia-Pacific capability.
4. Develop Strategy and MessagingPrepare Chinese-market positioning, factual support, corporate narrative, key messages, stakeholder plan, platform strategy, local approval route, advertising disclosures and crisis scenarios.
5. Classify Advertising, KOL Content and Data UseDetermine whether content directly or indirectly markets goods or services; identify advertiser, agency, KOL and platform roles; define “广告” labelling, claims, endorsement, personal information, targeted marketing and cross-border data controls.
6. Produce MaterialsDevelop Chinese media materials, executive briefings, Q&As, public affairs documents, investor messages, platform content, KOL agreements, advertising labels, claims files and personal information records.
7. Execute and EngageConduct media and platform relations, public affairs, corporate and investor communications, consumer and KOL campaigns, livestream activity, events, local stakeholder dialogue or crisis response.
8. Monitor and ReportMonitor media, platforms, social conversation, regulators, stakeholders, consumers and investors, together with claims, advertising labels, KOL conduct, data issues and reputational developments.
9. Review Risk and ChangeUpdate strategy, messaging, approvals, labels, data controls and crisis planning as corporate events, policy, platform rules, regulatory developments, stakeholder concerns or campaign performance changes.
Decision logic: Start with the business objective, city and regional footprint, Chinese target audience, sector and platform. Then select the relevant China communications service-line. If content markets a product or service directly or indirectly through an internet medium, treat it as a potential internet advertising activity: decide who is the advertiser, ensure recognisable and prominent “广告” labelling, validate claims and retain documentary control over KOL, agency and platform responsibilities before publication.

Foreign parent companies, regional teams, global agency networks, cross-border supply chains and international investors commonly shape the engagement. China content and campaign operations must nevertheless be assessed independently for Chinese language, local platforms, advertising labels, KOL compliance, sectoral restrictions, data localisation and cross-border personal information requirements.

Operating Constraints, Risks and Costs

The central practical risk is treating China as only a large translation market or an extension of global platform activity. Credible work requires Chinese-language quality, city and regional stakeholder analysis, platform-specific execution, strong factual and claims control, recognisable advertising, KOL governance and disciplined personal information management. The Internet Advertising Measures apply broadly across text, images, audio, video and other internet formats. Content that is commercial in substance but appears independent or editorial can create exposure for the advertiser, agency, publisher or platform.

Internet Advertising Identification RiskInternet advertisements must be recognisable and prominently marked “广告”. Content that directly or indirectly markets goods or services but is not identifiable as advertising can trigger correction and financial penalties under the advertising framework.
KOL and Endorser RiskKOLs and endorsers can carry personal exposure for false or misleading endorsements, while advertisers and agencies remain responsible for content direction, claims, disclosure, monitoring and campaign governance.
Platform and Format RiskPlatform tools, livestream disclosures, short-video labels, search advertising, pop-ups, algorithmic recommendations and e-commerce content can each require different implementation. A generic “#ad” approach may not be sufficient without local platform and legal assessment.
Claims and Restricted Sector RiskHealth, medical, pharmaceutical, food, financial, education, alcohol, cosmetic, environmental, performance and “best” or ranking claims can be restricted or require substantial evidence and sector-specific review.
Personal Information and Data RiskMedia, stakeholder, customer and KOL data, CRM, targeting, personalisation, analytics and cross-border transfers require PIPL, cybersecurity and data-security analysis. Automated marketing based on personal characteristics requires alternative non-personalised options or a convenient refusal mechanism.
Government, Regional and Crisis RiskA Beijing-only plan can miss Shanghai, Greater Bay Area or regional stakeholders; weak fact verification, unapproved content, delayed Chinese-language response or inconsistent local and global messaging can escalate a reputational issue rapidly.
Cost DriversSenior strategic counsel, Chinese content and translation quality, local media and public affairs access, platform operations, KOL governance, sector and claims review, data compliance, monitoring, regional coordination, executive training and crisis-response availability.

FAQ

Is public relations a regulated profession in China?No separate statutory licence applies to PR as a professional service-line. However, PR and communications activity is materially affected by advertising, consumer, internet, data, financial, media and sector-specific rules, and individual campaign formats can trigger specific regulatory requirements.
What is SAMR's role in communications and influencer marketing?SAMR administers the Advertising Law and Internet Advertising Measures. It is relevant to advertising claims, commercial content, KOL endorsements, internet advertising disclosure, consumer promotions and local market regulation enforcement.
Must KOL and influencer content be marked as advertising?Where online content directly or indirectly promotes goods or services as commercial advertising, it must be recognisable and prominently labelled “广告” so consumers can identify it as advertising. The advertiser should assess the actual relationship, payment or benefit, platform and content format before publication.
Who is responsible for compliant influencer advertising?Responsibility can extend to the advertiser, agency, KOL or endorser and platform according to their actual role. Brands should not treat labelling as the creator's isolated task; contracts, content approval, claim verification, platform implementation and monitoring should be governed centrally.
Does PIPL affect marketing and communications?Yes. PIPL governs personal information used in CRM, audience targeting, personalised marketing, media and stakeholder databases, analytics, creator management and cross-border transfers. Automated marketing based on personal characteristics must include a non-targeted option or convenient means to refuse.
